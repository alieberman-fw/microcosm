CITY COMPREHENSIVE PLAN 2040 — EXCERPTS RELEVANT TO Z-118-26
Adopted 2022 · Synthetic/demonstration

FUTURE LAND USE MAP (FLUM) DESIGNATION
The subject parcel carries TWO designations split at the 300-ft depth line:
- Milbrook Rd frontage band (front 300 ft): "CORRIDOR MIXED-USE" — supports
  4–7 stories, residential densities of 30–60 du/acre, ground-floor retail
  encouraged, on corridors with existing or funded high-frequency transit.
- Rear parcel area adjoining Ashford Grove: "NEIGHBORHOOD EDGE" — supports
  "gentle density" of 8–18 du/acre in house-scale forms (townhomes,
  duplex/quadplex, small apartment houses to 3 stories) with transitions.

POLICY LU-4.2 (TRANSITIONS): "Where Corridor Mixed-Use abuts established
single-family neighborhoods, plans should step height down to 3 stories or
less within 150 feet of the shared boundary and locate the most intense
uses on the corridor frontage."

POLICY H-1.1 (HOUSING SUPPLY): "The city shall support rezonings that add
housing supply within 1/2 mile of funded high-frequency transit corridors,
provided neighborhood transition policies are met." Milbrook BRT is a
FUNDED corridor (2029 service date) — the site qualifies.

POLICY H-2.3 (AFFORDABILITY): Contested rezonings above 20 du/acre "are
encouraged to include income-restricted units consistent with the council's
adopted target of 8–10% at 60–80% AMI."

PLANNING STAFF PRELIMINARY NOTE (pre-application conference memo, Nov 2025)
Staff advised the applicant that the FLUM split is the central issue:
"As filed, the 4-story massing extends approximately 120 feet into the
Neighborhood Edge band at 39 du/acre — inconsistent with LU-4.2 and the
Edge designation. A plan that holds 4 stories to the corridor band, steps
to 3 stories at the split line, and to house-scale forms within 100 feet
of the R-4 boundary could be found CONSISTENT with the plan as a whole.
Staff also notes council's recent pattern of requiring affordability
commitments on contested corridor cases."
