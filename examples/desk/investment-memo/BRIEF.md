# Desk — Juniper Row IC memo (investment memo)

The research-desk choreography: a director assigns memo sections by name,
analysts draft in parallel, the director merges into the executive summary.
The feed literally shows a memo assembling itself.

## PROBLEM STATEMENT (paste into the brief)

```
Draft the investment committee memo: acquire Juniper Row, a completed and
78%-leased 146-home build-to-rent community in Huntersville, North Carolina
(Charlotte MSA, fictional), from its merchant builder at a negotiated
$52.5M ($359,589/home). The seller needs to close inside 60 days for fund-
life reasons. Recommend: proceed at $52.5M, proceed only at a lower number
(name it), or pass. The memo must take positions — on basis vs. the
single-family comp stack, on the rent-growth story, on lease-up completion,
on financing, and on who the exit buyer is in 2031.
```

## QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE

1. `Is $359,589/home the right basis against both the BTR comp set and the
   scattered single-family resale stack the renters could buy instead?`
2. `Does the underwritten 3.4% rent growth survive the submarket's supply
   pipeline and the affordability ceiling of its renter base?`
3. `What does completing lease-up from 78% to 95% actually take — time,
   concessions, and the operating cost of getting there?`
4. `What financing clears today, and does the 60-day close constraint cost
   us basis or leverage?`
5. `Who buys this in 2031, at what cap rate, and what has to be true for
   that buyer to exist?`

## SUCCESS CRITERIA (one per Enter)

- A committed recommendation: proceed at $52.5M, proceed at $X, or pass
- Every memo section owned and drafted: basis, market, operations, financing, exit
- The three numbers the deal lives or dies on, stated plainly
- The seller's 60-day constraint priced: what discount does urgency justify?
- Open risks listed with the diligence item that would retire each one
