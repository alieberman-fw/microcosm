INDUSTRIAL OUTDOOR STORAGE (IOS) — ASSET CLASS PRIMER
Internal education memo · Copperline Analytics · Synthetic/demonstration

WHAT IT IS
Low-coverage industrial land (typically <20% building coverage) used for
truck/trailer parking, container storage, equipment yards, and contractor
laydown. Value is in the STABILIZED, ZONED, IMPROVED ACRE, not the shed:
paving, drainage, lighting, fencing, and a small office/maintenance
building. Institutional term of art since ~2020; national aggregate market
estimated at $200B+ across ~500K parcels, overwhelmingly private-owned.

WHY THE MOAT EXISTS
- Cities rarely zone FOR outdoor storage; most districts prohibit or
  condition it. Legal, conforming IOS is a shrinking set — municipalities
  routinely upzone IOS land to "higher and better" uses, removing supply.
- NIMBY dynamics run one way: yards get rezoned away; nobody rezones TO
  truck parking next to anything.
- Demand is structural: freight, e-commerce final-mile trailer pools,
  utility/infrastructure contractors, equipment rental fleets.

NATIONAL BENCHMARKS (2025, for calibration — verify locally)
- Rents: $2,500–6,000/acre/month depending on metro, paving, and power;
  coastal port markets at the top, interior metros $2,800–4,200
- Rent growth 2021–2025: +30–60% cumulative in tier-1 IOS metros
- Stabilized cap rates: 5.75–6.75% for credit-tenanted, paved sites in
  major metros; low-improvement sites trade on $/acre, not cap
- Yield-on-cost for buy-fix-lease (paving, power, fencing): 7.5–9.5%
- Typical institutional site: 3–15 usable acres, 85%+ paved, near
  interstate interchange or intermodal

RISKS THE PANEL SHOULD PRESSURE-TEST
1. CYCLICALITY: trailer-pool and construction-laydown demand both track
   freight volumes and construction starts; a simultaneous freight
   recession + construction slowdown can cut effective rents 15–25%
   (observed in two metros, 2023).
2. ZONING TWO-EDGED SWORD: the moat protects incumbents but blocks
   improvements — repaving or adding power can trigger site-plan review
   that reopens the nonconforming-use question.
3. ENVIRONMENTAL: legacy yards carry recognized environmental conditions;
   Phase II frequency in IOS diligence is roughly 3x warehouse deals.
4. EXIT DEPTH: the institutional buyer pool, while growing, remains thin
   in non-coastal metros — platform sales (20+ sites) have cleared better
   than onesies.
5. MEASUREMENT: "usable acres" vs. gross acres routinely differ 15–30%
   after setbacks, detention, and fire lanes — rent quotes must be tied
   to usable acres or comparisons are meaningless.
