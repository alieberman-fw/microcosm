# Expedition — Salt Lake City IOS market entry (research pack)

The autonomous deep-research choreography: scouts fan out through five
phases — questions → research → analysis → alternatives → verify — and the
feed reads as a findings log along the route. No crowd, no polls: pure
research.

## PROBLEM STATEMENT (paste into the brief)

```
Our firm is considering a $75M, 24-month allocation to build a position in
industrial outdoor storage (IOS) in the Salt Lake City metro (fictional
scenario). Before any deal work, build the research pack: what is the real
supply, demand, and pricing picture for IOS in this metro; where is the
zoning moat strongest; should we enter by aggregating small sites or buying
one platform; and what specific findings would kill the thesis? End with the
finding each scout would stake their name on.
```

## QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE

1. `How much legally usable IOS land actually exists in the metro, and which
   zoning districts create the scarcity moat?`
2. `Who is the demand — and how cyclical is it if construction and freight
   both slow at once?`
3. `What are current rents, recent trades, and realistic yield-on-cost for
   stabilized IOS here versus the national bid?`
4. `Aggregation of 3–8 acre sites versus one platform purchase: which entry
   strategy fits $75M over 24 months?`
5. `What would kill this thesis — name the specific, checkable tripwires.`

## SUCCESS CRITERIA (one per Enter)

- A quantified supply picture: acres, districts, and the permitting reality
- A demand map by user type with cyclicality honestly assessed
- Rent and pricing evidence separated into verified vs. broker-hearsay
- A committed entry recommendation: aggregate, platform, or do not enter
- Five kill-criteria tripwires, each tied to a measurable signal
