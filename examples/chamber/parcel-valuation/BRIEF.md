# Chamber — Coyote Flats parcel valuation (land & parcel valuation)

The flagship valuation use case (CLAUDE.md §2) on the mode built for it:
every expert values the parcel INDEPENDENTLY (no anchoring on the ask or on
each other), then blind peer review tears the workpapers apart, then the
chair synthesizes a defended range. This is how an appraisal panel should
work and almost never does.

## PROBLEM STATEMENT (paste into the brief)

```
Coyote Flats is a ±38.2-acre industrial-zoned land parcel at the northeast
quadrant of I-10 & Sarival Ave in Goodyear, Arizona (fictional), marketed at
$12.5M ($7.51/SF). We are evaluating it as a land-bank / build-ready
acquisition for a future 550,000 SF logistics development. What is the parcel
actually worth today? Triangulate sales comparison, residual land value, and
highest-and-best-use, argue the adjustments, and deliver a defended price
RANGE plus a walk-away number. The ask is an input to argue with, not an
anchor.
```

## QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE

1. `What does the comp set support on a $/SF basis once dates, sizes, and
   utility status are honestly adjusted?`
2. `What residual land value falls out of a 550K SF spec build at today's
   rents, costs, and exit caps — and how sensitive is it to the cap rate?`
3. `Is industrial still the highest and best use given the utility timeline,
   or does hold/flex/outdoor-storage beat it?`
4. `What is the sewer timing really — and what does every quarter of delay
   cost in carried value?`

## SUCCESS CRITERIA (one per Enter)

- A committed price RANGE in $/SF and total dollars, plus a single walk-away number
- Each of the three valuation approaches shown with its own answer before reconciliation
- The "shovel-ready" marketing claim tested against the utility letters, on the record
- A cap-rate and rent sensitivity note: what moves the range by more than 10%
- Preserved dissent: the lowest credible valuation and its full argument
