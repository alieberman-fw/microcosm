ARVADA WADSWORTH CORRIDOR — MARKET STUDY (EXECUTIVE SUMMARY)
Prepared by Copperline Analytics for Southgate Acquisition LLC · March 2026
All data synthetic/demonstration

1. MULTIFAMILY DEMAND
Within the 3-mile primary market area (PMA):
- Existing inventory: 8,420 market-rate units; stabilized occupancy 94.1%
- Under construction: 1,140 units across 4 projects (deliveries 2026–2027)
- New Class A effective rents (2025 leases): $1.98/SF average
  - Studio $1,620 ($2.31/SF) · 1BR $1,890 ($2.08/SF) · 2BR $2,410 ($1.83/SF)
- Concessions: 6 weeks free on new lease-ups, burning off at stabilization
- Absorption at the two most recent comparable lease-ups:
  - The Foundry (2024, 312 units): 21 units/month to 93%
  - Rail & Vine (2023, 268 units): 24 units/month to 95%
- PMA renter household growth forecast: +1,410 households/year through 2030,
  driven by G-Line access and Denver-core price displacement.
Conclusion: 900 units in two phases of ~450 is absorbable at 20–24 units/
month per phase IF phased at least 24 months apart. Underwriting above
$2.05/SF average requires a finish/amenity package exceeding current PMA
Class A, or a 3–5 year rent-growth bet. We model $1.95–2.05/SF at opening.

2. RETAIL REALITY CHECK
- PMA retail vacancy: 11.8%; corridor strip vacancy 15.2%
- Achieved NNN rents, new-construction street retail (six comps, 2023–2025):
  $22.00–$27.50/SF, average $24.75/SF
- Anchor-adjacent junior-box demand is weak: two backfills in the PMA took
  31 and 44 months to lease.
- Supportable NEW street retail at Southgate given trade-area leakage
  analysis: 60,000–85,000 SF at stabilization. A 120,000 SF program implies
  either drawing regional tenancy (unproven at this node) or 25%+ structural
  vacancy.
Conclusion: right-size retail to 70,000–80,000 SF anchored by food &
beverage and the existing GreenLeaf grocer.

3. RESIDENT SENTIMENT (SURVEY, n=402, PMA HOUSEHOLDS)
- 47% support redeveloping Southgate "as proposed or denser"
- 29% conditional: traffic mitigation and park delivery in Phase 1 are the
  two most-cited conditions (68% and 54% of conditional respondents)
- 18% oppose any residential above 3 stories on the site
- Owners within 1/2 mile skew oppose (41% oppose) vs. renters (9% oppose)

4. COST ENVIRONMENT
- Podium multifamily hard costs, Denver metro Q1-2026: $242–265/SF
- Anchor demolition with slab/foundation removal and asbestos abatement in
  1970s retail stock has run $19–24/SF on three recent metro projects —
  materially above typical seller estimates.
