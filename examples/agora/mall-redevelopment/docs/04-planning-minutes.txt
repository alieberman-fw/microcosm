CITY OF ARVADA — PLANNING COMMISSION, MEETING MINUTES (EXCERPTS)
Regular session, February 11, 2026 · Item 4: Wadsworth Corridor implementation
All names and proceedings synthetic/demonstration

STAFF PRESENTATION (Planning Director T. Okafor)
Staff reviewed implementation status of the 2023 Wadsworth Corridor Vision
Plan. Director Okafor clarified, in response to Commissioner Ruiz, that the
Vision Plan is ADVISORY: parcels within the corridor retain their underlying
zoning, and residential densities above 20 du/acre on commercially zoned
parcels require a REZONING to the MX-U district with full public process —
neighborhood meeting, commission recommendation, and city council approval.
Staff noted average MX-U rezoning timelines of 10–14 months over the past
three years.

ITEM 4a — 6800 WADSWORTH MIXED-USE (280 UNITS) — CONTINUED SECOND TIME
The commission continued the 6800 Wadsworth application (280 units, 5
stories) for a second time, citing an incomplete traffic mitigation plan.
Public comment summary: 22 speakers; 15 opposed, 5 support, 2 neutral.
Recurring themes from opponents (Maple Leaf and Sierra Vista neighborhoods):
- Cut-through traffic on 70th Ave and Balsam St ("already unbearable at
  school dismissal" — M. Deering, Maple Leaf HOA president)
- Building height transition to single-family lots ("five stories looking
  into my yard" — 3 speakers)
- School capacity at Fitz Elementary (cited by 6 speakers; district letter
  states capacity exists through 2031)
Commissioner Vance: "This corridor plan only works if applicants arrive with
the neighborhood already at the table. I will not advance projects that
start that conversation here."

COMMISSIONER DISCUSSION OF SOUTHGATE SITE (NO APPLICATION PENDING)
Commissioner Ruiz asked staff whether the Southgate Mall site would face the
same process. Director Okafor: yes — any residential program at Southgate
requires MX-U rezoning; however, staff views Southgate as "the corridor
plan's centerpiece" and would support density there IF the application
includes (1) a signalized intersection and turn-lane package at 72nd Ave,
(2) a public park delivered in the first phase, and (3) a height taper to
3 stories within 150 feet of the western property line.
Chair Whitfield noted "significant latent goodwill toward fixing Southgate —
residents hate the dead mall more than they fear apartments — but the
applicant who gets this done will have knocked on a thousand doors first."
