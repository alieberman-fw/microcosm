# Agora — Meridian Tower office-to-residential conversion (go/no-go)

The most argued-about trade in CRE, in one building: a half-empty 1986 tower
priced at land value, a conversion pro forma that only works if three
optimistic claims survive contact with the architect, the rent comps, and the
city's actual incentive terms. Engineering, demand, and public-money seats
all get real material; the adversarial seat gets a feast.

## PROBLEM STATEMENT (paste into the brief)

```
Meridian Tower is a 22-story, 310,000 SF Class B office tower (built 1986) at
4th & Commerce in downtown Riverport (fictional mid-size Midwest city), 71%
vacant after two anchor-tenant departures. Seller asks $14M ($45/SF). We are
underwriting a full conversion to approximately 240 market-rate apartments
with ground-floor retail, leaning on the city's Downtown Conversion Incentive
Program. The broker's teaser pencils the deal at $185/SF conversion cost and
$1.95/SF rents. Should we buy at $14M and convert, renegotiate, or pass? The
IC wants a committed answer with the load-bearing numbers named — not a list
of considerations.
```

## QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE (add each; the chip label auto-summarizes)

1. `Does the floor plate actually convert — what do the core-to-window depths
   on floors 4–12 do to unit count, layouts, and cost?`
2. `What is the honest all-in basis — acquisition plus real conversion cost —
   and what does that make each finished unit cost?`
3. `Can downtown Riverport absorb 240 more units at the rents we need, with
   three competing conversions in the pipeline?`
4. `What does the city's incentive program actually require — is the
   abatement as-of-right, and what do its conditions cost us?`
5. `What is the exit — who buys or refinances a stabilized conversion in this
   market, and at what value per unit?`

## SUCCESS CRITERIA (one per Enter)

- A committed BUY / RENEGOTIATE / PASS with the price that changes the answer
- A defended all-in cost per unit range, reconciling the teaser vs. the feasibility study
- A rent and absorption position tied to the market study, not the teaser
- The incentive math shown both ways: with and without the abatement conditions
- Three tripwires that would flip the recommendation, each with a watch signal
