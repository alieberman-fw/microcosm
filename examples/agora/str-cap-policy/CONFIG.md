plausibly convert to long-term housing under a cap?`

housing-vs-lodging fight.)

## Demo beats (what to point at)

- **The signature fight**: the housing needs assessment estimates ~60% of
  capped-out STRs would return to year-round housing; the lodging
  association's study says 8% (second homes stay second homes). The registry
  extract carries the data to split the difference — purpose-of-ownership,
  occupancy, and owner domicile by neighborhood. Watch the economists build
  a defended middle estimate and cite it.
- **Planted contradiction (ownership)**: the lodging study describes the
  market as "predominantly local, owner-occupied home-sharing"; the registry
  shows 58% of whole-home licenses held by out-of-county LLCs and the top
  ten owners holding 31%. Someone reads the registry aloud.
- **The crowd as instrument**: this example runs the biggest resident crowd
  (400). Watch the per-round poll shift as the amendment ideas land —
  support for PASS vs. AMEND should visibly move between rounds 2 and 4.
- **Interjections**: bustling density pulls six residents into the feed per
  round — harbor-district hosts, out-commuting teachers, hotel staff.
- **Take the Floor** at the pause:
  - `@<municipal counsel> — does grandfathering-without-transfer survive a takings challenge in this state's framework?`
  - `@<finance director> — what does the town lose in lodging-tax revenue under the cap, and what does enforcement cost?`
- **Report**: expect AMEND with a specific package (transferability window,
  home-share carve-out, enforcement funding, phased cap). The consent map
  section should read like a whip count by neighborhood.
