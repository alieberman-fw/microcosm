HARBORVIEW HOUSING NEEDS ASSESSMENT (EXECUTIVE SUMMARY)
Prepared for the Town of Harborview by Meridian Community Planning
All data synthetic/demonstration

1. THE AFFORDABILITY GAP
- Median home price (trailing 12 mo): $612,000. Median household income:
  $71,400. Price-to-income ratio: 8.6 (state benchmark: 4.9).
- Long-term rental vacancy: 1.9% (healthy range 5–7%). Median asking rent
  for a 2BR: $2,340 — requiring ~$94K household income at 30% of income.
- 68% of Harborview's hospitality, school, and municipal workers now live
  outside town; out-commuting is up 34% since 2019.

2. THE STR SHIFT
- Active whole-home STR licenses: 1,058 (≈19% of ~5,600 dwelling units) —
  among the highest shares on the coast.
- Units converted from year-round occupancy to STR since 2019: an estimated
  640, based on utility-billing patterns, school enrollment by address, and
  license issuance dates. Conversions concentrated in the Harbor District
  (31% of its housing stock) and Eastport (24%).
- Over the same period the town permitted only 187 new dwelling units.

3. WHAT A CAP COULD RETURN
Of the ~386 licenses above the proposed 672 cap (attrition via
non-transfer at sale and lapse):
- We classify 60% AS PLAUSIBLE YEAR-ROUND CANDIDATES: units that were
  year-round occupied within the last six years, are winterized, and sit in
  neighborhoods with year-round services. We estimate the balance (~40%)
  remains second homes or seasonal.
- At historical sale turnover (6.1%/yr) the cap returns an estimated
  ~23 units/year to the year-round market in years 1–5 — modest, but equal
  to 74% of the town's average annual new-unit permitting.
- Sensitivity: if the candidate share is 35% (skeptic case), the return is
  ~13 units/year; at 75% (advocate case), ~29 units/year.

4. RENT EFFECT
Cross-town comparison of five coastal peers that adopted caps (2021–2024)
shows 2BR asking-rent growth 2.1–3.4 points BELOW un-capped peers over the
following two years. We caution that no peer matches Harborview exactly.

5. RECOMMENDATION
Adopt the cap WITH a home-share exemption (as drafted) and pair it with
by-right ADU permitting and the workforce-housing overlay — the cap alone
does not close the gap; it stops the bleeding.
