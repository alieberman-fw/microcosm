THE ECONOMIC CONTRIBUTION OF SHORT-TERM RENTALS IN HARBORVIEW
Commissioned by the Harborview Lodging & Hospitality Association
Prepared by Bayside Economic Consulting · All data synthetic/demonstration

KEY FINDINGS
- STR guests contributed an estimated $84.2M in direct visitor spending
  last year: $31.6M lodging, $27.4M food & beverage, $25.2M retail,
  recreation, and services.
- This spending supports an estimated 1,120 JOBS (direct + indirect) and
  $4.1M in combined local tax receipts, including $2.9M in lodging tax.
- STRs house 58% of Harborview's overnight visitors; hotel and inn capacity
  (612 keys) cannot absorb displaced demand in peak season, which runs at
  96% hotel occupancy July–August.

THE MARKET, AS WE UNDERSTAND IT
Harborview's STR community is PREDOMINANTLY LOCAL, OWNER-OCCUPIED
HOME-SHARING and long-held family cottages — hosts renting to meet carrying
costs, not investment operators. Caps punish these families first.

PROJECTED IMPACT OF ORDINANCE 26-04
- Licenses eliminated over 5 years (via non-transfer at sale): ~386.
- Assuming displaced visitor-nights are NOT recaptured by hotels in peak
  season, we project a 22% REDUCTION IN STR VISITATION, equal to $18.5M/yr
  in lost direct spending and ~245 jobs by year 5.
- Lodging-tax loss to the town: ~$640K/yr by year 5 — 4.1% of the general
  fund.

ON "RETURNING UNITS TO HOUSING"
We estimate ONLY 8% of capped-out units would convert to year-round
rentals. The remainder are second homes whose owners will simply hold them
vacant or use them personally: seasonal properties do not become workforce
housing by ordinance. Claims that hundreds of units will return to the
long-term market are not supported by owner surveys conducted for this
study (n=142 self-selected association members; 11 said they would consider
a year-round tenant).

ALTERNATIVES TO A CAP
The Association supports: registration and platform enforcement (Sections
4–5 of the ordinance), a good-neighbor code with real penalties, and a
voluntary seasonal-to-year-round incentive program — without the cap.

METHODOLOGY NOTE
Spending figures derive from platform gross-booking data scaled by an
industry-standard 1.9 spending multiplier; employment via IMPLAN-style
coefficients. Survey instrument and coefficients available on request.
