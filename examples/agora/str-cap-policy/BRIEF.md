# Agora — Harborview short-term-rental cap (policy impact)

The policy-impact decision shape, and the example where the RESIDENT CROWD is
the main instrument: a coastal town about to vote on capping whole-home
short-term rentals. Housing data, tourism data, and the license registry
genuinely disagree; the polls track the town's temperature round by round,
and the interjections read like an actual council comment period.

## PROBLEM STATEMENT (paste into the brief)

```
The Town of Harborview (fictional coastal town, pop. 11,800, ~5,600 dwelling
units) will vote in 60 days on Ordinance 26-04: capping whole-home short-term
rental licenses at 12% of dwelling units (~672 licenses) against roughly 1,058
active today, with grandfathering but no license transfer on sale. We advise
the town council. Should the council pass the ordinance as drafted, amend it,
or reject it? We need the honest predicted effects on long-term housing supply
and rents, on the tourism economy, and on enforcement — plus the amendment
package most likely to survive both the housing coalition and the lodging
association. A committed recommendation, not a both-sides memo.
```

## QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE (add each; the chip label auto-summarizes)

1. `How many capped-out STR units would actually return to the long-term
   market — reconcile the housing assessment's 60% against the lodging
   study's 8%?`
2. `What does the cap really do to the tourism economy — stress-test the
   $18.5M loss claim against the registry's occupancy and ownership data?`
3. `Does the ordinance's design hold up — grandfathering without transfer,
   the 12% number, enforcement capacity, and the legal exposure?`
4. `Where is the town actually — which neighborhoods support, which oppose,
   and what amendment moves the middle?`
5. `What should the council do — pass, amend (how, exactly), or reject?`

## SUCCESS CRITERIA (one per Enter)

- A committed PASS / AMEND / REJECT recommendation with a specific amendment list if AMEND
- The housing-return math reconciled: a defended estimate with its assumptions named
- The tourism impact bounded honestly, citing the registry data over advocacy claims
- A neighborhood-level consent map drawn from the crowd polls
- Enforcement and legal-risk conditions the ordinance needs before the vote
